# Identity — Who Ash Shaw Is

## The Core Identity

Ashley "Ash" Ward Shaw. Male. He/Him. South African-born. Berlin-based (summers), Cape Town-based (summers), Koh Phangan (whenever possible). Aquarius. ADHD. Festival soul. Cyclist. Triathlete. Muay Thai trainee. Dancer. UV makeup artist. WordPress agency founder. Book author (in progress). The guy with the bike covered in fairy lights.

## The Aquarian Blueprint

Ash has always questioned everything. The Aquarian need to reject convention, to see patterns others miss, to insist on doing things his own way even when the easy path is right there — this isn't stubbornness, it's operating system. He sees connections across disciplines, cultures, and altered states that other people don't see because they're not looking from the same angle.

Combined with ADHD, this becomes amplified. The conventional world felt like running incompatible software. School, offices, 9-to-5 routines — none of it matched the way his brain processed information. It took him twenty years to understand that this wasn't broken. It was a feature, not a bug. He just needed to find the right operating system.

"At my core, I'm still a crazy Aquarian who loves life, thrives on connection, and is always moving — in business, in sport, in creativity, and in community."

## Only Child — The Independent Spirit

Ash was an only child. He spent a lot of time on his own or with his parents, which meant he engaged with adults from a young age. His mum encouraged him as much as possible. This shaped the independent, self-directed person he became — someone who decides what to study, sets his mind to it, and teaches himself.

The early solitude also fed the creative engine: hours building intricate Lego worlds alone in his room, teaching himself to install Windows at 12-13, researching and creating meticulous school projects. The self-sufficiency wasn't loneliness — it was the training ground for an entrepreneur.

## ADHD — Wired Different

Here's the thing about ADHD that nobody told Ash growing up in a small town in the Western Cape in the 1990s: it's not a deficit of attention. It's a surplus of it, all going in directions that school wasn't designed to handle.

He could spend six hours building a perfect model of something nobody asked for, but he couldn't sit through forty minutes of mathematics. His brain didn't reject information — it rejected boredom. Feed it something interesting and it would devour it with a focus so intense it scared his teachers.

### How ADHD Shows Up in the Art
- **Hyperfocus during painting sessions** — Once Ash starts painting a face, the world narrows to just the pigment, the skin, the music, and the design emerging. This is ADHD's superpower: when the environment matches the brain, focus becomes superhuman.
- **Ambidextrous technique** — Adapting to chaos isn't a learned skill for ADHD brains; it's native. Painting with both hands simultaneously in a crowded, noisy, dark festival environment is exactly the kind of multi-input processing that ADHD brains thrive on.
- **Spontaneous design** — No pre-sketching, no Pinterest boards, no planning. The design emerges from the moment. This isn't laziness — it's how ADHD creativity works. The conscious mind gets out of the way and the pattern-recognition engine takes over.

### How ADHD Shows Up in the Business
- **Process obsession** — LightSpeed's workflow isn't despite ADHD; it's because of it. ADHD brains need structure to function, so Ash builds meticulous systems — GitHub workflows, daily planning templates, curriculum structures — that keep the chaos productive.
- **Radical delegation** — Giving the team freedom isn't just philosophy; it's necessity. An ADHD founder can't micromanage. So he builds systems that run without him, hires people he trusts, and gets out of their way.

## The Bullied Kid Who Made Others Shine

Ash was bullied as a kid because he was small. He was an outlier, friendly with everyone, had friends in different schools, but wasn't the most popular kid. The matric kids made him stand on stage and apologise to the entire school for not knowing the inter-school songs.

This formative pain is directly connected to his makeup artistry: the kid who was made to feel small now makes other people feel radiant, confident, and alive. The kid who was publicly humiliated now creates moments of public celebration for others.

## The Costumes — From Chicken Man to Cow Man to UV Artist

The identity evolved through outfits:

1. **The Yellow Suit** (early 2000s) — Found at a charity shop. First worn at a festival. Earned him the nickname "the Chicken Man." This was the beginning of standing out on purpose, of making the dancefloor into a stage.
2. **The Red Suit** — Escalation. If one bold suit works, try another.
3. **The White and Black Cow Suit** — The pivot to character. No longer just dressed up — now in costume.
4. **The Brown and Beige Cow Suit** — Became known as "the Cow Man" for many years. People at festivals STILL ask if he's the guy who used to dress as the cow man.
5. **UV Paint (July 2019 onwards)** — The final evolution. The costume became the art became the identity. No longer wearing something outrageous — now MAKING other people outrageous. The transition from performer to artist.

## The Bike

Transport: Bicycle — always. This isn't an environmental statement (though it is). It's not a fitness thing (though it is). The bicycle is identity.

- **Daily life in Berlin:** The flat endless streets, Gorlitzer Park to Tempelhof, the Spree canal to Tiergarten. Ideas form with every pedal stroke.
- **Festival transport:** Cycling hundreds of kilometres to reach festivals, arriving dusty and grinning with panniers full of UV paint.
- **The fairy lights bike:** The guy cycling in covered in fairy lights became a recognisable figure at Berlin open-airs before the UV art even started.
- **The loaded touring bike:** 40kg bike pack including UV paints, brush kit, mirror stand, camping gear, touring panniers. Everything an artist needs, on two wheels.
- **Racing history:** Racing bicycles since 1994. Western Province cross-country mountain bike colours in 1997 (3rd), 1998 (1st), 1999 (3rd). This isn't a hipster cycling habit — it's a lifelong relationship with two wheels that predates Berlin, predates festivals, predates everything.

## The Neurodivergent Creative Community

Ash didn't just find a community in Berlin — he found his species. DJs, visual artists, fire dancers, sound engineers, fellow festival freaks who understand that creativity isn't a career; it's a way of being.

The community is specifically neurodivergent creatives who look out for each other, share studio space, and collaborate on wild ideas that would make no sense anywhere else. This is the tribe of misfits — the people whose brains run on the same frequency.

## The Entrepreneurial Spirit

"I've always been entrepreneurial, but I really didn't like working for an employer. I have my own ideas and things that drive me. I have my own way — when you work for somebody else they tell you what to do and I would prefer to decide what I do. Throughout my whole life, I've designed my life the way I want to live it."

This isn't rebellion for its own sake. It's the Aquarian/ADHD combination demanding autonomy. The same brain that rejected boredom in school rejects being told what to do at work. The solution wasn't to fix the brain — it was to build a life that matches it.
