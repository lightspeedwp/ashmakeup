## The Discovery (2019)

Ash discovered Berlin in 2019, just before COVID shut the world down. He didn't arrive with years of planning — it came through the usual channel: stories whispered at afterparties, faces painted by people who'd been there, the collective word-of-mouth of the festival circuit that always points eventually to Berlin.

He arrived with a bike and the instinct that this city was going to mean something. He was right.

What he found was a city that doesn't just tolerate difference — it demands it. From Kreuzberg's graffiti-covered facades to Neukölln's late-night shawarma runs, Berlin gave him permission to be unapologetically himself.

---

## The COVID Pause and the Return (2022)

The pandemic interrupted everything. Berlin went quiet, the season that had barely started was cancelled, and Ash returned to Cape Town — his permanent home — and sat out the years that swallowed everyone's plans.

In 2022, he came back. Some venues were gone. Some people had moved on. But the energy was still there, rebuilding itself the way Berlin always does. He picked up exactly where he'd left off: bike, UV paints, open-airs, and the slow warm unfurl of a Berlin summer.

---

## The Seasonal Pattern

Berlin is a summer chapter, not a full-time life. The rhythm is consistent:

- **Late May:** Arrive. The city is waking up from winter. Open-airs just starting.
- **June–August:** Peak season. Park gatherings, canal parties, techno nights, festival weekends in Brandenburg. The longest days, the warmest nights, the best of what Berlin does.
- **September (at the latest):** The outer edge. The city belongs to the locals as the tourist crowds thin. Still some of the best parties of the year. But it's time to head home.
- **The departure:** Pack up. The Berlin bike stays. Fly back to Cape Town.

## What Berlin Means Creatively

The darkness of Berlin's clubs is the perfect canvas for UV work. The looks here are sharper, more geometric, edgier than festival looks — they need to cut through strobe lights and industrial atmosphere. The contrast mirrors the city itself: green parks and concrete warehouses, Sunday morning brunches and Monday morning afterhours.

He started painting faces at small open-airs in Hasenheide Park, then techno nights in Friedrichshain. Word spread through the scene — the guy with the UV paints and the bike covered in fairy lights.

## Sisyphos

Sisyphos is his "little piece of heaven" in Berlin — the venue that almost always delivers. Over the summers he's met incredible people there, reconnected with old friends, and made memories that define his Berlin experience. It was at Sisyphos that he met Aixa Reynosa.

## What Berlin Gives Him

1. **Freedom to be weird** — Not just tolerated but expected. A city where cycling to a club in fairy lights with a box of UV paints is completely unremarkable.
2. **The right scene** — Techno and psytrance communities that overlap, collaborate, and welcome outsiders. The underground isn't gatekept.
3. **A found family** — A community of neurodivergent creatives, DJs, visual artists, fire dancers, and festival freaks who look out for each other.
4. **Creative fuel** — Every Berlin summer produces a leap in skill and output that the rest of the year runs on.

## The Departure Ritual

When September comes, Berlin is packed down. The bike stays. Ash flies back to Cape Town, his base. From there, if the year calls for it, the next chapter begins: swap bikes, plan the Thailand leg.
