# Berlin — Personal History

## Arrival (2016)

Berlin called to Ash not through brochures or travel blogs, but through stories whispered at afterparties and painted across the faces of returning festival friends. He arrived with a one-way ticket, a bike, and exactly enough savings for two months' rent.

What he found was a city that doesn't just tolerate difference — it demands it. From Kreuzberg's graffiti-covered facades to Neukolln's late-night shawarma runs, Berlin gave him permission to be unapologetically himself.

## The Underground Scene

Berlin's club culture is legendary, and for good reason. The city's warehouses, abandoned power stations, and basement bars became Ash's studios. Under UV lights and surrounded by pounding techno, he discovered that makeup art and electronic music are two expressions of the same energy.

He started painting faces at small open-airs in Hasenheide Park, then at techno nights in Friedrichshain. Word spread through the scene — the guy with the UV paints and the bike covered in fairy lights.

## Bike Life in Berlin

Ash doesn't just cycle in Berlin — he lives on his bike. It's his primary transport, his meditation, and sometimes his stage. He's cycled to festivals hundreds of kilometres away, arriving paint-laden and road-dusted.

There's something about the flat, endless Berlin streets that frees the mind. Between Gorlitzer Park and Tempelhof, between the Spree canal and Tiergarten, ideas form with every pedal stroke.

## The Berlin Family

Berlin attracts misfits, dreamers, and artists from everywhere. Ash found his tribe here — DJs, visual artists, fire dancers, sound engineers, and fellow festival freaks who understand that creativity isn't a career; it's a way of being.

The community he's built isn't just friends at parties. It's a support network of neurodivergent creatives who look out for each other, share studio space, and collaborate on wild ideas that would make no sense anywhere else.

## Berlin Summers

If you haven't experienced a Berlin summer, you're missing one of Europe's great secrets. From May onwards, the city transforms into one continuous open-air event. Parks become stages, canals become bars, and every sunset feels like it was art-directed.

For Ash, summer in Berlin means painting faces at open-airs until 3am, cycling home through warm streets that smell of linden trees, and knowing there's another party tomorrow. And the day after. And the day after that.

## Berlin's Club Culture as Canvas

The darkness of Berlin's clubs is the perfect canvas for UV work. Ash creates sharper, edgier looks that cut through the strobe lights and industrial atmosphere. The contrast between daylight art (soft, organic, approachable) and club art (sharp, geometric, aggressive) mirrors the city's own duality — green parks and concrete warehouses, Sunday morning brunches and Monday morning afterhours.

## Why Berlin Specifically

Berlin gave Ash three things no other city could:

1. **Freedom to be weird** — Not just tolerated but expected. A city where cycling to a club in fairy lights with a box of UV paints is completely unremarkable.
2. **Affordable creative space** — Compared to London, Paris, or San Francisco, Berlin let a young South African build a life without the crushing overhead that kills creativity.
3. **The right scene** — Techno and psytrance communities that overlap, collaborate, and welcome outsiders. The underground isn't gatekept — it's an open door for anyone who shows up with energy and sincerity.

## Berlin in the Seasonal Calendar

- **May–August:** Primary base. Open-air season. Park gatherings, canal parties, cycling to nearby festivals in Brandenburg. The longest days and the warmest nights.
- **September:** Shoulder season. Some of the best parties happen as the crowds thin. The city belongs to the locals again.
- **Returning after Cape Town season:** The feeling of landing at BER, getting on the bike, and cycling into the city. The smell of linden trees. The sound of distant bass from someone's balcony. Home.

## Sisyphos — The Little Piece of Heaven

Sisyphos holds a special place in Ash's Berlin life. It's his "little piece of heaven" — the one place where a good time is almost guaranteed. Over the years he's met incredible people there, reconnected with old friends, and made memories that define his Berlin experience. It was at Sisyphos that he met Aixa Reynosa.
