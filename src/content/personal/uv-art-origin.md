# UV Art — The Origin Story

## The Moment It Started

**July 2019, Berlin.** A psytrance gathering somewhere in the city. Ash picked up UV-reactive face paint for the first time. Something clicked that had been building for twenty years of festival life — from the yellow Chicken Man suit, through the Cow Man era, through years of being the most visible person on every dancefloor — but never having an art form of his own.

That night, the art found him.

## From Costume Wearer to Artist

The evolution was:
1. **Dressing up** — Standing out on the dancefloor through outfits (1999–2019)
2. **Being seen** — Becoming a recognisable figure at parties (the guy in the suit, the guy on the fairy-light bike)
3. **Creating** — Turning OTHER people into the spectacle (July 2019 onwards)

The shift from performer to artist was the final piece. Twenty years of dancefloor immersion, of understanding what makes someone light up under UV, of reading energy and crowd dynamics — all of it was preparation for picking up that first brush.

## The Creative Process

### No Plan, Just Feeling
Ash doesn't sketch beforehand. He doesn't look at Pinterest boards or follow trends. His creative process begins with a feeling — the energy of the music, the light conditions, and the vibe of the person sitting in front of him.

This isn't chaos; it's trust. Trust in thousands of hours of practice, in the muscle memory of his ambidextrous hands, and in the belief that the best art happens when you stop trying to control it.

### The Steps
1. **Read the energy** — Before touching paint, Ash reads the person. Their energy, their outfit, their vibe. The music playing. The light. Everything informs the design.
2. **Choose the palette** — Based on skin tone, outfit colour, and the UV conditions of the venue, he selects 3–5 neon pigments that will create maximum impact under blacklight.
3. **Base layer** — A sponge-applied gradient base, often blending two neon colours, establishes the foundation. This goes on fast while the energy is high.
4. **Detail work** — Fine brushes create the patterns: geometric shapes, organic flowing lines, third-eye detailing, psychedelic fractals. This is where the ADHD hyperfocus kicks in.
5. **The reveal** — The moment under blacklight when the full design comes alive. Colours that seemed subtle suddenly blaze with neon intensity. This is the magic moment.
6. **Capture & release** — A quick photo or video under UV, then back to the dancefloor. The art lives on the skin until dawn, in photos forever, and in the memory of everyone who saw it glow.

### The Tools
- UV-reactive face paints — specifically formulated to pop under blacklight while remaining skin-safe for hours of dancing
- High-pigment neon palettes
- Fine-detail brushes
- Sponge applicators for base gradients
- Everything fits in a single bag that straps to the bicycle
- Portability isn't a limitation — it's a design constraint that forces creative efficiency

### Ambidextrous Application
Festival energy moves fast, and so does Ash. He's trained himself to be ambidextrous, allowing him to paint with flow and precision even in the most crowded, high-energy environments. This isn't a party trick — it's a practical adaptation to painting in chaos. Symmetrical designs, third-eye patterns, complex geometric work — all done with both hands, simultaneously, on a moving dancefloor.

## Painting for the Reveal, Not the Application

UV art has a unique property: what you see during application is NOT what you see under blacklight. Colours that look muted in daylight become explosively vivid under UV. Understanding this transformation is central to Ash's process — he paints for the reveal, not the application.

This requires a mental map of how each pigment transforms. Certain greens that look bland in daylight become nuclear under UV. Pinks that seem aggressive in natural light become ethereal. The experienced UV artist is painting a version of reality that doesn't exist yet — and trusting the blacklight to complete the work.

## What UV Art Means

"I don't just paint faces; I unlock avatars. I focus on eyes and face because that's the centre of connection on the dancefloor."

After the transformation, the person isn't just a spectator; they're part of the visual spectacle of the event. Ash captures the moment, and they stay connected through the global tribe.

## The Connection to Expanded Awareness

The connection between expanded states of awareness and UV art is direct. When Ash paints someone's face, he's not decorating — he's translating. Translating the visual language of heightened perception into pigment that anyone can see, especially under blacklight.

Certain transformative experiences permanently upgraded the resolution at which he perceives the visual world. The way neon paint bleeds under UV light, the way colours breathe and pulse on a dancefloor — these are things Ash noticed because his awareness had been opened to what was always there.

Every neon stroke carries a piece of those experiences. Every glowing face on the dancefloor is a small portal into that wider, more vivid way of seeing. That's the real art — not the paint, but the perception it represents.

## UV Mascara Techniques

### Blonde Eyebrows
Ash has blonde eyebrows, which means you don't really see them normally. When he puts UV mascara on, suddenly he has eyebrows — they pop under blacklight in a way that transforms his face completely.

### Multiple Mascara Colours
He uses multiple colours of mascara on his eyelashes, creating a layered, multicoloured effect that's striking under UV light. This is a subtle technique that creates quite a nice effect.

### Core UV Kit
- UV mascara (essential — transforms blonde features)
- UV eyeliner
- A UV torch to check what the UV makeup looks like during application
- UV paints and pens in multiple shades
- These three items never leave home

## Collaboration & Reading Faces

### The Process
- Tries a variety of approaches — listens to what the person might want
- Uses imagination to create what they describe
- They don't see what he's doing; he sees what he's doing
- Gets ideas looking at their complexion or facial features
- Something that works on one person may not work on another
- Co-creating is amazing — he really enjoys that collaborative approach

### Colour & Music Connection
- Depending on the type of music, it may impact the colour palette
- The music playing influences mood, which influences creative choices

## Consent, Hygiene & Photography

### Before Painting
- Always tries to wash his hands beforehand for hygiene reasons
- Asks people what their likes and dislikes are
- Reads their comfort level and energy

### Photography
- At festivals or places where he can take photos, always asks if they'd mind a photo at the end
- Uses his **Google Pixel phone** — takes incredible portrait photos
- Festival photos are essential to the portfolio since Berlin club nights are camera-free

## Pricing & Value

### Not for Profit
- Doesn't charge people for makeup
- Sometimes people offer money; sometimes he tells them they can buy him a drink
- "For me it's the pleasure of being able to do my art on somebody and see them happy afterwards"
- This is a personal art project, not a professional pursuit
- "I'm creating a makeup portfolio website primarily so that I can showcase the cool stuff that I've done, but I'm not going to pursue it as a professional career. It is part of my party experience. I love doing it at festivals and making other people happy, as well as doing my own makeup and getting creative."

### The Joy of Making People Shine
- An experience recently: saw someone after not seeing them for two years, had done their makeup two years ago
- That day, they felt extremely special — everyone kept complimenting them and it made their day
- This is the lasting impact: the memory of feeling radiant stays with people

## Fusion Nails

### The Concept
- Layered, colourful nail art designs combining nail varnish, stickers, and topcoats for a unique finish
- Named "Fusion Nails" by Ash for the layered technique

### The Process
- Sources most stickers from DM in Germany
- Layers stickers over colour blends
- Seals with a glossy topcoat
- Sometimes subtle, sometimes bold — when they come together, the results can be stunning
