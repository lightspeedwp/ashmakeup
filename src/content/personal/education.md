# Education & School

## Early Childhood — Formative Experiences

### The Lego Years
Ash spent hours alone in his room building intricate Lego systems — towers, complex multi-level structures spanning an entire bookshelf that became his Lego world. The Lego men had systems to move up and down different levels. He would spend hours amusing himself, completely absorbed. This was the first sign of both the ADHD hyperfocus and the creative engineering mind that would later build WordPress design systems and UV art installations.

### The First Computer (Age 12-13)
Ash's dad gave him a gold computer. He needed to set it up with Windows himself. He went to a friend Guy for a bolt and got hold of Windows 3.1 installation discs on stiffy drives — about 12 discs to install Windows. He taught himself how to install it, then taught himself how to strip the computer and put it back together. He made a few mistakes along the way. His friend Ron from up the road helped him resolve some hardware issues and taught him how to troubleshoot basic computer hardware maintenance problems. This was the beginning of the self-taught tech journey.

### Parents' Values
Ash's parents modelled values that still drive him today:
- Honesty
- Hard work
- Dedication
- Good business ethics
- A good, honest set of morals

### Only Child
Ash was an only child. He spent a lot of time on his own or with his parents, which meant he engaged with adults from a young age. His mum encouraged him as much as possible to grow into the person he is today.

### Early Chores & Jobs
- Collecting snails in the garden as a child — his parents would pay him a few cents per snail
- These early tasks taught responsibility and the basic concept of earning

### Miss Scott — The Teacher Who Saw Potential
Miss Scott saw the potential in Ash. She realised he was a special person and encouraged him throughout junior school. She first came into contact with him in Standard 1, then again in Standard 3, Standard 4, and Standard 5. She was their English and History teacher. In a school system that didn't know how to handle his ADHD brain, she was the teacher who did something differently — she recognised what was there and encouraged it.

### School Projects — The Early Maker
Ash took enormous pride in his school projects in junior school. He used the computer to type out and print sections, then glued them onto backgrounds of coloured paper and covered them with plastic to protect them. He used to go to the library and research things — he loved doing research. This meticulous approach to projects (using available technology, researching thoroughly, presenting beautifully) foreshadowed his later career in web design and development.

### ADHD in the School System
Ash grew up with undiagnosed ADHD in a small town in the Western Cape in the 1990s. The school system wasn't designed for brains like his:
- Could spend six hours building a perfect model of something nobody asked for, but couldn't sit through forty minutes of mathematics
- Brain didn't reject information — it rejected boredom
- Feed it something interesting and it would devour it with a focus so intense it scared his teachers
- It took him twenty years to understand this wasn't broken — it was a feature, not a bug
- "I just needed to find the right operating system."

### Bullying
Ash was bullied as a kid because he was small. He was a bit of an outlier or outcast — friendly with everyone, had friends in different schools, but wasn't the most popular kid. Nobody really relied on him; they just used to bully him.

One particularly painful memory: he was made to stand up on stage and apologise to the entire school for not knowing the inter-school songs. He still cannot remember lyrics. The matric kids made him stand in front of everyone. This kind of public humiliation shaped his empathy and his later commitment to making others feel seen and valued — the exact opposite of what was done to him.

### The "2 O'Clock Club"
According to his school, Ash was known as the "2 o'clock club" because at 2pm he went home — to train on his bicycle. While other kids stayed for after-school activities, he was already on the road.

### Sport at School
While the classroom was a struggle, the sports field was where Ash thrived:

- Represented Western Province cross country mountain bike team
- Western Province XC MTB colours: 1997 (3rd), 1998 (1st — champion), 1999 (3rd)
- Racing bicycles since 1994 — sport was never optional, it was survival

## Philosophy on Tertiary Education

**"I've never really believed in tertiary education — it's not for me."**

Ash likes deciding what he's going to study, setting his mind to it, and then studying it. He's taught himself all the skills he has over the years through self-study. He believes that anything you learn at university is outdated by the time you graduate — even more true in today's world with AI.

He wouldn't say he's made any bad study choices. All of the things he's studied along the way have contributed various skills towards his overall knowledge. He never did any extended tertiary education, so he didn't make any wrong choices spending years studying something he didn't want to do as a career.
